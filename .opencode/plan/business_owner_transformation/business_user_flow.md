# OpenCode Business Owner User Flow Blueprint

**Document Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** January 16, 2026  
**Purpose:** Define ideal user flows for business owner transformation  
**Classification:** Strategic Planning Document

---

## Executive Summary

This document defines the comprehensive user flow blueprint for transforming OpenCode from a developer-focused AI coding agent to a business owner-focused commerce operations platform. The user flows outlined here represent the ideal experience for business owners across their entire journey—from initial discovery through daily operations, campaign execution, strategic decision-making, and long-term platform growth.

The blueprint addresses seven core flow categories that together create a cohesive, outcome-oriented experience: Onboarding Flow, Daily/Weekly Operations Flow, Campaign/Project Flow, Decision-Making Flow, Reporting Flow, Interaction Patterns, and Exit/Scale Flow. Each flow is designed to eliminate friction, provide immediate value, and build confidence through progressive disclosure of capabilities.

The user flows are grounded in three key principles derived from competitive analysis and business requirements research. First, business owners think in outcomes, not tasks—the system must translate business objectives into automated execution without exposing technical complexity. Second, business owners need confidence, not control—they want to make informed decisions with clear recommendations rather than being forced to become technical experts. Third, business owners operate in time-boxed rhythms—the system must align with quarterly planning cycles, campaign timelines, and daily operational patterns rather than requiring users to adapt to system architecture.

## 1. Onboarding Flow

The onboarding flow represents the critical first impression that determines whether a business owner becomes a long-term user or abandons the platform within minutes. Research indicates that the "aha moment"—the point at which a user understands the platform's value—must occur within the first five minutes of interaction. For business owners, this moment typically comes when they see their own data presented in a clear, actionable format that answers their most pressing questions.

### 1.1 Discovery and Initial Engagement

Business owners discover OpenCode through multiple channels: word of mouth from peers, online searches for e-commerce management solutions, marketplace recommendations, and advertising. Regardless of discovery channel, the initial engagement must communicate a clear value proposition tailored to business owners. The messaging emphasizes outcomes—"Grow your e-commerce business with AI-powered insights and automation"—rather than technical capabilities.

Upon first visiting the platform, the business owner encounters a streamlined landing page that avoids technical jargon entirely. The page focuses on three core value propositions: unified business visibility across all sales channels, AI-powered recommendations for growth, and automated content creation for marketing. Each value proposition includes a brief example relevant to e-commerce: "See all your sales—from Shopify to Amazon to your own website—in one dashboard" or "Get product descriptions, ad copy, and social posts created automatically."

The call-to-action invites the business owner to "Connect your store and see your business in a new way." This framing positions the next step as a connection to their existing business rather than a registration for a new tool. The frictionless approach minimizes barriers: no credit card required, no lengthy form to complete, immediate access to core functionality after connection.

### 1.2 Connection and Data Import

The connection process represents the first critical interaction point where the system demonstrates value. Rather than presenting a technical configuration screen, the connection flow uses a conversational, wizard-based approach that guides the business owner through linking their data sources.

**Step 1: Store Connection**

The business owner sees a simple interface presenting the platforms they likely use: Shopify, Amazon, WooCommerce, and other major e-commerce platforms. The interface uses familiar branding and icons rather than technical terminology. Clicking a platform icon initiates a standard OAuth connection flow that users recognize from countless other applications.

The system handles all technical aspects—API authentication, permission requests, data synchronization—without exposing complexity to the user. Upon successful connection, the system provides immediate feedback: "Connected to your Shopify store. Syncing your products, orders, and customer data." This feedback reinforces success while setting expectations for the next step.

**Step 2: Secondary Connections (Optional)**

After the primary store connection, the system presents optional connections for marketing platforms (Meta Ads, Google Ads, Klaviyo, Mailchimp), analytics tools (Google Analytics, Triple Whale), and financial systems (QuickBooks, Xero). Each option is presented with clear value communication: "Connect Google Ads to track your advertising ROI" or "Link QuickBooks to see profit margins alongside revenue."

The optional nature of these connections respects the business owner's time while establishing a path for deeper integration. The system emphasizes that even without these connections, they can immediately begin seeing value from their primary store data.

**Step 3: Historical Data Load**

Upon connection, the system loads historical data to provide immediate context. This includes past sales data, product performance metrics, customer acquisition trends, and marketing campaign results. The loading process displays a progress indicator with contextual messaging: "Loading your sales history... Analyzing your top-performing products... Understanding your customer patterns."

This historical analysis serves a dual purpose: it prepares the system to provide personalized insights, and it demonstrates value immediately by surfacing patterns the business owner may not have previously recognized. The messaging emphasizes findings in business-relevant terms: "Your best-selling product in Q4 generated 34% of your revenue" or "Customers who bought Product A had a 2.3x higher repeat purchase rate."

### 1.3 Immediate Questions Answered

Within moments of completing connection, the system proactively answers the questions every business owner asks when first exploring a new platform. These questions are presented not as a FAQ but as natural conversational responses that demonstrate understanding of the business owner's priorities.

**Question 1: "How is my business performing?"**

The system presents a high-level health score alongside key metrics: total revenue for the current period, year-over-year growth, and profit margin trend. The health score provides an immediate answer to the performance question without requiring the user to interpret raw numbers. Supporting metrics appear in expandable sections for those who want deeper detail.

**Question 2: "What products are driving my business?"**

The system surfaces top-performing products with clear performance indicators: revenue contribution, growth trend, and margin analysis. Each product appears with a brief insight: "This product represents 28% of your revenue and has grown 15% month-over-month."

**Question 3: "Where should I focus my attention?"**

The system identifies three to five priority items based on the business owner's data: potential inventory risks, underperforming campaigns that could be optimized, and opportunities for growth. Each item includes a one-sentence explanation of why it matters.

**Question 4: "What can this platform do for me?"**

Rather than a technical feature list, the system presents three specific examples of immediate value: "See your complete business performance in one view," "Get AI recommendations for improving your marketing ROI," and "Create product descriptions and ad copy in seconds."

### 1.4 Guided Setup and Goal Configuration

After answering initial questions, the system guides the business owner through optional setup that personalizes the experience. This setup is presented as helping the system "understand your business better" rather than configuring technical parameters.

**Goal Setting Conversation**

The system initiates a natural language conversation: "To help you get the most value, I'd like to understand your goals. What does success look like for your business this quarter?" The business owner responds in plain language—perhaps "I want to grow revenue by 20%" or "I'm focused on improving my profit margins" or "I need to launch three new products."

The system translates these goals into specific targets and explains how it will help: "Got it—growing revenue by 20% this quarter. I'll track your progress, identify opportunities, and suggest actions to help you get there. Based on your historical data, that would require approximately $47,000 in additional revenue, which means reaching about 850 new customers or increasing average order value by 12%."

**Notification Preferences**

The system asks how the business owner wants to stay informed: "How would you like me to keep you updated? Daily summaries of your performance? Weekly progress reports? Alerts when something needs your attention?" Options include email, in-app notifications, SMS for urgent matters, or a weekly digest.

**Team and Access (Optional)**

For business owners who work with team members, the system offers to set up team access: "Would you like to add team members who can also view and manage your business? You can invite them now or do this later from your settings."

### 1.5 The Aha Moment

The aha moment occurs when the business owner sees their own business data transformed into clear, actionable insights that answer their most pressing questions. This moment must be carefully engineered to occur within the first interaction.

The specific aha moment varies by business owner persona. For the e-commerce entrepreneur overwhelmed by fragmented data across platforms, the aha moment comes when they see all their sales channels unified in a single view with clear attribution. For the retailer struggling to understand true profitability, the aha moment comes when they see profit margins calculated automatically with clear cost breakdown. For the marketing manager pressured to demonstrate ROI, the aha moment comes when they see campaign performance linked directly to revenue with clear return calculations.

The aha moment is reinforced by a prompt from the system: "This is just the beginning. I can help you with so much more—optimizing your advertising, creating marketing content, planning new product launches, and more. What would you like to explore next?"

## 2. Daily/Weekly Operations Flow

The daily operations flow defines how business owners interact with the platform during regular business activities. This flow must balance providing continuous value with respecting the business owner's limited time. Research indicates that business owners typically spend 15-30 minutes daily reviewing their business metrics and 1-2 hours on strategic activities weekly. The platform must deliver maximum value within these time constraints.

### 2.1 Daily Dashboard Experience

The first screen a business owner sees upon logging in is their personalized dashboard—the "morning briefing" that provides a complete picture of business health. This dashboard follows a hierarchical structure that allows quick scanning for anomalies while enabling deep dive into areas of interest.

**Executive Summary Section**

The dashboard opens with a prominent executive summary displaying three key elements: today's revenue compared to target, a health indicator showing any areas requiring attention, and a progress bar toward the current week's goals. The summary uses natural language rather than raw metrics: "You're 12% toward your weekly revenue goal. Your advertising ROAS is trending above target. One inventory item needs attention."

**Key Metrics Bar**

Below the summary, a horizontal metrics bar displays the five most important numbers for the day: total revenue (with comparison to yesterday and last week), number of orders, average order value, conversion rate, and customer acquisition cost. Each metric includes a small trend indicator showing direction: a green up arrow for positive trends, a red down arrow for concerning trends, and a gray horizontal line for stable metrics.

**Alert Section**

Any items requiring attention appear in a dedicated alert section with clear prioritization. Critical alerts (stockouts, significant performance drops, large orders requiring approval) appear first with prominent formatting. Opportunity alerts (underutilized promotional opportunities, competitor activity, emerging trends) follow. Each alert includes the business impact and a recommended action: "Your best-selling product is 85% stocked. Reorder now to avoid stockout estimated to cost $2,300 in lost sales."

**Recent Activity Stream**

A scrollable activity stream shows recent events across all connected systems: new orders, customer inquiries, advertising performance updates, and social media mentions. This stream provides context without requiring the user to check multiple platforms.

### 2.2 Natural Language Queries

Business owners interact with the platform primarily through natural language queries rather than navigating complex menus or building reports. The query interface is always accessible and optimized for the types of questions business owners actually ask.

**Query Patterns by Category**

Business owners ask questions across several predictable categories, and the system is optimized to recognize and respond to each:

Performance queries focus on understanding how the business is performing: "How are sales this week compared to last week?" "What's my ROAS for Facebook Ads?" "Which products are my top performers this month?" The system responds with clear answers, supporting visualizations, and relevant context—"Your ROAS is 3.2x, which is above your 30-day average of 2.9x and above the industry benchmark of 2.5x."

Comparison queries focus on understanding differences across dimensions: "How does this month's revenue compare to last month?" "What's the difference in conversion rate between mobile and desktop?" "How are my holiday campaign ads performing compared to regular campaigns?" The system provides side-by-side comparisons with clear explanations of the drivers behind any differences.

Trend queries focus on understanding patterns over time: "What's the trend in my customer acquisition cost over the past 6 months?" "How has my repeat purchase rate changed since launching the loyalty program?" "What's the trajectory for my new product launch sales?" The system presents trend data with period-over-period analysis and projection where relevant.

Forecasting queries focus on understanding future expectations: "What revenue should I expect this month based on current trends?" "How much inventory will I need for the holiday season?" "What's my projected profit for Q1?" The system provides data-driven forecasts with confidence intervals and identified risks.

**Query Response Format**

Every query response follows a consistent format designed for business owners. First, a direct answer to the question in plain language. Second, supporting data presented in clear visualizations appropriate to the query type. Third, relevant context that helps interpret the data. Fourth, recommended actions if the response suggests opportunities or risks. Fifth, suggested follow-up queries for users who want to explore further.

### 2.3 Actionable Insights Delivery

Beyond answering direct queries, the system proactively delivers insights that help business owners improve performance. These insights are delivered through a combination of dashboard presentation, notifications, and a dedicated insights inbox.

**Insight Categories**

Performance insights highlight changes in key metrics: "Your conversion rate dropped 8% this week. The drop is concentrated in mobile traffic. This typically indicates a mobile usability issue. Would you like me to analyze your mobile checkout flow?"

Opportunity insights highlight potential improvements: "Your Facebook Ads ROAS has increased 23% since you updated creative last week. Applying this winning creative to your Google Ads could potentially improve your overall ROAS by 15%. Want to try it?"

Risk insights highlight potential problems: "Your inventory for Product X will run out in 4 days based on current sales velocity. This product contributes 12% of your revenue. Reordering now would prevent an estimated $4,500 in lost sales."

Competitive insights highlight market dynamics: "A competitor launched a sale on similar products with 20% discounts. Your share of voice has decreased 15% this week. Would you like recommendations for maintaining your market position?"

**Insight Prioritization**

Not all insights are created equal, and the system prioritizes based on business impact and urgency. Critical insights requiring immediate attention trigger notifications and appear prominently on the dashboard. Important insights requiring attention within days appear in the insights inbox. Valuable insights worth reviewing when time permits appear in a "For Your Consideration" section.

The system also learns from business owner behavior over time, understanding which types of insights are most valuable to each user and adjusting prioritization accordingly.

### 2.4 Delegation and Automation

Business owners can delegate routine tasks to the system through clear delegation mechanisms. This delegation follows natural language patterns: "Keep an eye on my inventory and alert me when anything gets low" or "Monitor my advertising ROAS and automatically pause ads that drop below 2.0x."

**Automation Rules**

The system supports creating automation rules through conversational setup: "When [trigger], then [action]." Triggers include inventory thresholds, performance metrics, time-based events, and competitive actions. Actions include notifications, automated adjustments (within defined limits), and approval requests for significant changes.

Each automation rule includes clear explanation of what will happen and requires explicit confirmation before activation. The system provides a dedicated automation management view where business owners can see all active rules, review their history, and make adjustments.

**Campaign Delegation**

For marketing campaigns, the system supports comprehensive delegation: "Run a retargeting campaign for cart abandoners" or "Launch a product launch campaign for my new item." The system handles all aspects of campaign execution within defined parameters, providing updates on progress and seeking approval for significant budget allocations.

**Content Delegation**

For content creation, the system supports creative delegation: "Create product descriptions for my new items" or "Generate social posts for this week's promotions." The system produces content that matches brand voice and style, presents options for review, and publishes upon approval.

### 2.5 Weekly Review Ritual

The weekly review represents a dedicated time for the business owner to step back from daily operations and assess progress toward goals. The system facilitates this ritual through a structured weekly review workflow.

**Weekly Report Delivery**

Every Monday morning, the system delivers a weekly report via the business owner's preferred channel. This report includes: weekly performance summary against goals, notable achievements and concerns, progress toward monthly and quarterly targets, and recommended focus areas for the coming week.

The report is designed for quick consumption—business owners can review the executive summary in under two minutes while having the option to drill deeper into any area of interest.

**Weekly Planning Session**

The system prompts a weekly planning session: "Would you like to review your progress and plan for the coming week?" This session follows a structured agenda: review last week's performance, assess goal progress, identify priorities for the coming week, and schedule any planned campaigns or initiatives.

The session is conversational rather than form-based, allowing business owners to engage naturally while ensuring all important topics are addressed. The system takes notes and creates action items that appear in the daily dashboard until completed.

## 3. Campaign/Project Flow

The campaign/project flow defines how business owners create, execute, and measure marketing campaigns and business initiatives. This flow represents a core value proposition—the ability to execute complex, multi-channel campaigns through natural language requests while maintaining control over strategy and creative direction.

### 3.1 Campaign Initiation

Campaigns begin with a natural language request that expresses the business owner's objective. The system engages in a brief clarifying conversation to understand key parameters, then generates a comprehensive campaign plan for review and approval.

**Request Patterns**

Business owners initiate campaigns through various patterns:

Goal-based requests express the desired outcome: "I want to increase sales for my spring collection" or "I need to generate more leads for my consulting services." The system responds by asking clarifying questions about the target audience, timeline, and budget constraints before proposing a campaign plan.

Competitive requests respond to market dynamics: "My competitor just launched a big sale and I need to respond" or "A new competitor entered my market with lower prices." The system proposes competitive response campaigns tailored to the specific situation.

Seasonal requests align with calendar events: "I want to do a big promotion for Black Friday" or "Create a Valentine's Day campaign for my gift shop." The system brings relevant seasonal context and historical performance data for similar past campaigns.

Product-based requests focus on specific items: "I just got new inventory for Product X and need to promote it" or "I have $5,000 of excess inventory I need to clear." The system proposes campaigns optimized for the specific product and inventory situation.

**Clarifying Conversation**

Based on the initial request, the system asks clarifying questions to understand parameters:

Target audience questions: "Who are you trying to reach? Are these existing customers or new prospects?"  
Timeline questions: "When do you want this campaign to run? Do you have a specific launch date?"  
Budget questions: "What's your budget for this campaign? Is there a maximum you're comfortable spending?"  
Channel questions: "Which channels do you want to use? Facebook, Instagram, Google, email, or all of the above?"  
Creative questions: "Do you have specific messaging or branding you want to use, or should I create something new?"

These questions are presented conversationally rather than as a form, and the system suggests reasonable defaults based on historical data: "Based on your past campaigns, your best-performing audience for new product launches has been lookalikes of your top 5% customers. Should I use that, or try something different?"

**Campaign Plan Generation**

After gathering parameters, the system generates a comprehensive campaign plan including:

Campaign overview: "This is a 14-day promotional campaign to drive sales of your spring collection, targeting existing customers and lookalike audiences, with a budget of $2,500 distributed across Facebook, Instagram, and email."

Channel strategy: Breakdown of approach by channel with rationale: "Facebook and Instagram will focus on awareness and consideration with carousel ads showing product benefits. Email will focus on conversion with personalized product recommendations based on purchase history."

Budget allocation: Specific budget amounts per channel with expected ROI: "$1,500 to Facebook/Instagram (expected ROAS: 3.2x), $500 to email (expected ROAS: 8.5x), $500 contingency for optimization."

Creative approach: Summary of creative strategy: "I'll create fresh product images and lifestyle shots, emphasizing the spring theme and sustainability messaging you mentioned."

Timeline: Detailed timeline with milestones: "Day 1: Launch awareness phase. Day 3: Introduce consideration messaging. Day 7: Launch retargeting and email sequences. Day 14: Evaluate and optimize."

Success metrics: Clear KPIs for measuring campaign success: "Target ROAS: 3.0x, Target CPA: $45, Target Revenue: $7,500."

### 3.2 Campaign Review and Approval

The campaign plan appears in a dedicated review interface that presents all information clearly while making it easy to approve, modify, or request changes.

**Plan Presentation**

The campaign plan is presented in a structured, scannable format:

Executive summary at the top with the core proposition and key numbers. Detailed sections below covering each aspect of the plan. Visual timeline showing the campaign progression. Budget breakdown with channel allocation. Expected outcomes with confidence levels.

**Modification Options**

Business owners can request modifications through natural language: "Can you increase the email budget since that has the best ROAS?" or "I want to focus on new customer acquisition, not existing customers." The system updates the plan and shows the impact of any changes: "Increasing email budget to $800 reduces Facebook allocation to $1,200. Based on channel capacity, this could reduce total expected revenue by approximately $300."

**Approval Process**

Once satisfied with the plan, the business owner approves with a single click: "This looks good—launch it." The system confirms receipt of approval and provides a launch timeline: "Campaign launching now. You'll see initial results within 2 hours. I'll send you a progress update this evening."

For campaigns with significant budget, the system requests final confirmation before committing funds: "Ready to launch. This will commit $2,500 from your advertising budget. Confirm to proceed."

### 3.3 Campaign Execution

Upon approval, the system executes the campaign across all specified channels, handling technical complexity while keeping the business owner informed of progress.

**Creative Generation**

The system generates all required creative assets through the integrated Spaces system:

Product images are created using product data and brand context. Ad copy is generated matching brand voice and campaign messaging. Email templates are produced with personalized product recommendations. Social content is created for each target platform.

All creative is generated in parallel where possible, with the system managing dependencies (product images needed before ad copy in some cases). The business owner can review all creative before publication or approve automatically based on preferences.

**Platform Configuration**

The system handles all platform configuration:

Advertising platforms receive campaign settings, creative, targeting, and budgets. Email platforms receive audience lists, templates, and sending schedules. Tracking systems receive conversion events and attribution settings.

The business owner sees confirmation of each connection: "Facebook Ads campaign created and submitted for review. Should be active within 1 hour." The system handles any platform-specific requirements or restrictions.

**Execution Monitoring**

Throughout campaign execution, the system monitors performance and provides updates:

Hourly updates for the first 24 hours: "Your campaign has been live for 6 hours. You've spent $340 with 12 conversions (ROAS 3.1x). This is slightly above target. No action needed—I'll continue monitoring."

Daily updates during active campaigns: "Day 3 of your spring collection campaign. You've spent $1,100 with 42 conversions (ROAS 3.4x). Facebook is outperforming expectations while email is slightly below. I'm testing some adjustments."

Milestone notifications: "You've reached 50% of your campaign budget with 60% of conversions generated—tracking ahead of target."

### 3.4 Campaign Optimization

Beyond passive monitoring, the system actively optimizes campaign performance based on real-time data.

**Automatic Optimizations**

Within defined parameters, the system makes automatic optimizations: "I noticed your Facebook ad with the lifestyle image is outperforming the product-only image 2.3x. I'm shifting $200 of budget to the winner." These optimizations are logged and reported.

**Optimization Recommendations**

For optimizations requiring judgment, the system presents recommendations: "Your Google Ads ROAS has dropped to 2.1x (target is 2.5x). The drop is concentrated in the 'running shoes' keyword group. I recommend either pausing this keyword or lowering bids by 15%. What would you like to do?"

**A/B Testing Management**

For campaigns with testing components, the system manages the testing process: "I've set up 4 ad variations as planned. After 48 hours, I'll analyze results and recommend a winner. Current early indicators suggest Variation B is leading with 15% higher click-through rate."

### 3.5 Campaign Review and Learning

After campaign completion, the system generates a comprehensive review that measures performance against goals and extracts learnings for future campaigns.

**Performance Summary**

The campaign review includes:

Goal achievement: "You set a target ROAS of 3.0x with expected revenue of $7,500. Actual ROAS was 3.2x with revenue of $8,200—you exceeded targets by 7% on ROAS and 9% on revenue."

Channel performance breakdown: "Facebook delivered 3.1x ROAS on $1,400 spend. Email delivered 8.2x ROAS on $500 spend. Instagram delivered 2.8x ROAS on $600 spend."

Creative performance: "The lifestyle image carousel outperformed product-only images by 34%. The sustainability messaging outperformed feature-focused messaging by 18%."

**Learnings Extraction**

The system identifies actionable learnings: "Three key learnings from this campaign: 1) Your email list is highly responsive—consider increasing email frequency. 2) Sustainability messaging resonates with your audience—use in future campaigns. 3) Instagram underperformed—investigate creative format or consider reallocating budget."

**Recommendations for Next Steps**

The review concludes with forward-looking recommendations: "Based on this campaign's success, I recommend: 1) Retargeting the 2,300 visitors who engaged but didn't purchase—estimated ROAS 4.5x. 2) Creating a similar campaign for your summer collection. 3) Testing the winning Facebook creative across Google Ads."

## 4. Decision-Making Flow

The decision-making flow supports business owners through strategic choices by providing relevant data, scenario analysis, and actionable recommendations. This flow recognizes that business owners often face complex decisions with incomplete information and need a trusted advisor to help navigate options.

### 4.1 Strategic Question Framing

When business owners face important decisions, the system helps frame the question clearly before diving into analysis.

**Question Clarification**

Through conversational dialogue, the system helps clarify the decision at hand:

Business owner: "I'm thinking about raising my prices."  
System: "Help me understand the context. Are you considering a price increase for all products or specific items? What's driving this consideration—rising costs, market positioning, or something else?"  
Business owner: "My costs have gone up 15% and I want to maintain my margins."  
System: "Got it. So the goal is cost recovery while maintaining conversion. How much of a price increase are you considering? Are you concerned about customer reaction?"

This clarification process ensures the subsequent analysis addresses the actual decision rather than a simplified version.

**Decision Structure**

The system structures the decision with clear components:

Decision: The specific choice to be made (price increase amount, product launch timing, marketing budget allocation).  
Options: The alternatives being considered (no change, 5% increase, 10% increase; launch now vs. next month vs. next quarter).  
Criteria: The factors that matter for the decision (revenue impact, profit margin, customer retention, competitive position).  
Constraints: Any limitations on the decision (minimum margin requirements, maximum price points, timing dependencies).

### 4.2 Data and Analysis Provision

Once the decision is framed, the system provides relevant data and analysis.

**Historical Data**

The system surfaces relevant historical data: "Here are the relevant historical patterns. Last year, you tested a 10% price increase on a subset of products. Conversion dropped 8% but margin improvement more than compensated—overall revenue increased 4%. Your elasticity analysis suggests a 5% increase would have minimal conversion impact while improving margins significantly."

**Comparative Analysis**

For decisions involving trade-offs, the system provides clear comparison: "Option A: 5% increase. Expected revenue impact: -2% (slight volume decrease). Expected margin impact: +12%. Risk: Low. Option B: 10% increase. Expected revenue impact: -8% (moderate volume decrease). Expected margin impact: +22%. Risk: Medium. Option C: No change. Expected margin impact: -5% (cost increase eats margin). Risk: Low short-term, medium long-term."

**Scenario Modeling**

For complex decisions, the system builds scenario models: "Let me model different scenarios based on market conditions. In a growth scenario where demand remains strong, a 10% price increase yields +18% profit. In a stable scenario, it yields +8% profit. In a competitive scenario where competitors don't follow, it yields -3% volume but +14% profit. The key risk is whether competitors follow—if they don't, you likely maintain volume."

### 4.3 Recommendation Delivery

The system provides clear recommendations with supporting rationale.

**Recommendation Format**

Each recommendation follows a consistent format:

Recommendation statement: "I recommend a 7% price increase."  
Supporting analysis: "This balances cost recovery with conversion preservation. Based on your historical elasticity and current market position, this should maintain conversion within 1-2% while improving margins enough to offset cost increases."  
Risk assessment: "The main risk is competitor response—if two major competitors also raise prices, you're fine. If one doesn't, you may see slightly more volume loss. I recommend monitoring competitor pricing closely for two weeks after the change."  
Implementation guidance: "I recommend implementing the increase on Monday, notifying customers through your newsletter, and monitoring conversion closely for the first 72 hours. I can help with the customer communication if you'd like."

**Confidence Expression**

The system expresses confidence levels honestly: "I'm 75% confident in this recommendation. The main uncertainty is competitor behavior—if you have insight into competitor pricing plans, that would improve my recommendation."

**Alternative Presentation**

When there are reasonable alternatives, the system presents them: "If you're more conservative, a 5% increase would still improve margins 8% with minimal conversion risk. If you're willing to accept more uncertainty, a 10% increase maximizes margin improvement but requires close monitoring."

### 4.4 Risk Evaluation

The system helps business owners understand and evaluate risks associated with decisions.

**Risk Identification**

The system identifies relevant risks: "The key risks of this decision are: 1) Customer price sensitivity—some customers may be lost if they perceive poor value. 2) Competitor response—if competitors don't follow, you may lose share. 3) Market conditions—if demand softens, the price increase compounds the impact."

**Risk Quantification**

Where possible, risks are quantified: "Based on historical data, a 7% price increase typically results in 1-3% conversion decrease. At your current volume, that represents $1,500-4,500 in monthly revenue risk. However, the margin improvement represents $4,200 in monthly profit benefit. The net expected impact is positive in 80% of similar situations."

**Mitigation Strategies**

The system suggests risk mitigation: "To mitigate customer risk, consider a grandfathering approach where existing customers keep the old price for 30 days. To monitor competitor risk, I can set up alerts for competitor pricing changes. To manage market risk, consider a flexible return policy that removes purchase barriers."

### 4.5 Decision Tracking and Learning

After decisions are made, the system tracks outcomes and learns for future recommendations.

**Decision Logging**

All decisions are logged with supporting context: "Price increase decision: 7% increase on Product Category X. Made on [date] based on cost pressure and margin goals. Predicted impact: +$4,200 monthly profit. Confidence: 75%. Review scheduled: 30 days."

**Outcome Tracking**

The system tracks actual outcomes against predictions: "30 days after price increase: Conversion decreased 1.5% (predicted 1-3%). Revenue impact: -$1,200 (volume decrease) + $3,800 (margin improvement) = +$2,600 net. Prediction accuracy: Good. Actual fell within expected range."

**Recommendation Improvement**

Over time, the system improves recommendations based on outcome data: "Based on your price increase outcome, I've updated my understanding of your customer price sensitivity. Future recommendations will reflect this learning—your customers appear less sensitive than typical, suggesting room for additional pricing optimization."

## 5. Reporting Flow

The reporting flow defines how business owners receive regular updates, create reports for stakeholders, and share insights with their team. This flow recognizes that business owners need to communicate business performance to multiple audiences with different information needs.

### 5.1 Regular Updates

Business owners receive regular updates through their preferred channels at appropriate frequencies.

**Daily Brief**

The daily brief arrives each morning and focuses on the previous day's performance and immediate priorities. Content includes: yesterday's revenue and key metric summary, any alerts or actions needed today, progress toward weekly goals, and a brief forward-looking statement about what to expect.

The daily brief is designed for quick consumption—under two minutes to review—while providing all essential information for starting the business day.

**Weekly Report**

The weekly report arrives Monday morning and provides broader perspective. Content includes: weekly performance against all goals, notable achievements and concerns, trend analysis showing trajectory, progress toward monthly and quarterly targets, and recommended focus areas for the week.

The weekly report is more comprehensive but still scannable—business owners can get the key points in five minutes while having the option to explore deeper.

**Monthly Review**

The monthly review provides comprehensive performance analysis. Content includes: complete monthly performance across all metrics, goal progress at month-end, trend analysis and forecasting updates, competitive position assessment, and strategic recommendations for the coming month.

The monthly review supports strategic reflection and planning, providing the data foundation for monthly business reviews.

**Quarterly Business Review**

The quarterly review provides strategic perspective on business health. Content includes: quarter performance against annual goals, progress toward long-term objectives, strategic insights and market analysis, annual projection updates, and recommendations for the coming quarter.

The quarterly review supports the primary business planning cycle, providing comprehensive data for strategic decision-making.

### 5.2 Stakeholder Reports

Business owners need to communicate performance to various stakeholders—investors, board members, partners, or team members—with different information needs.

**Report Customization**

The system helps create customized reports for different audiences:

Investor reports focus on growth trajectory, market opportunity, and capital efficiency. The system highlights: revenue growth rate, customer acquisition trends, market share indicators, and path to profitability.

Board reports focus on strategic progress and key decisions needed. The system highlights: goal achievement, major initiatives status, risks and mitigations, and decisions requiring board input.

Team reports focus on operational metrics and execution. The system highlights: sales performance, customer satisfaction, operational efficiency, and team productivity.

Partner reports focus on collaborative success and joint opportunities. The system highlights: shared customer performance, co-marketing results, and partnership opportunities.

**Report Generation**

Reports are generated through simple requests: "Generate an investor report for Q4" or "Create a board presentation for our February meeting." The system produces professionally formatted reports with clear visualizations and appropriate depth.

**Report Customization**

Business owners can customize report content through natural language: "Focus the investor report on our customer acquisition efficiency and lifetime value improvements" or "For the board report, emphasize our competitive position and market share gains."

### 5.3 Team Collaboration

Business owners share insights with their teams through integrated collaboration features.

**Insight Sharing**

Individual insights can be shared with team members: "Share this advertising optimization recommendation with my marketing manager" or "Send this inventory alert to my operations lead." The system handles distribution and tracks acknowledgment.

**Report Distribution**

Regular reports can be automatically distributed to team members: "Send the weekly performance report to my management team" or "Distribute the monthly review to all department heads." Distribution includes appropriate formatting for each audience.

**Collaboration Features**

Team members can add comments and questions to shared insights: "Marketing Manager: We tried this last quarter with different results—what's different now?" The system facilitates discussion while keeping the business owner informed of team engagement.

**Permission Management**

The system supports role-based access: "Give my operations manager view access to inventory and fulfillment data" or "Allow my marketing coordinator to create and edit campaigns but not change budget." Permissions are managed through natural language commands.

## 6. Interaction Patterns

This section defines the natural language patterns, dashboard designs, notification strategies, and feedback mechanisms that create a cohesive user experience across all flows.

### 6.1 Natural Language Query Patterns

Business owners interact with the platform primarily through natural language. The system must understand and respond to the full range of business-owner queries.

**Query Categories and Examples**

Performance queries ask about business results:  
"How are my sales doing?"  
"What's my ROAS this week compared to last week?"  
"Which products are my top 5 performers?"  
"How does my conversion rate compare to last month?"  
"What's my profit margin trend for Q4?"

Comparison queries ask about differences:  
"How does this week's revenue compare to last week?"  
"What's the difference in performance between my Facebook and Google ads?"  
"Which customer segment has higher lifetime value—new or returning?"  
"How am I performing compared to my competitors?"

Planning queries ask about future actions:  
"What should I focus on next quarter?"  
"How much inventory do I need for the holiday season?"  
"When should I launch my new product?"  
"What's the best budget allocation for my spring campaign?"

Explanation queries ask for understanding:  
"Why did my conversion rate drop this week?"  
"Why are my advertising costs increasing?"  
"What's driving my customer acquisition cost up?"  
"Why are returns higher on this product?"

Recommendation queries ask for advice:  
"What should I do to improve my ROAS?"  
"How can I increase my average order value?"  
"What products should I promote more?"  
"Where should I invest my marketing budget?"

**Query Response Patterns**

Every query response follows a consistent structure:

Direct answer in plain language. Supporting data with appropriate visualization. Context that helps interpret the data. Recommended actions if relevant. Suggested follow-up queries for deeper exploration.

### 6.2 Dashboard Design Patterns

Dashboards provide visual summaries of business performance with clear hierarchy and progressive disclosure.

**Dashboard Hierarchy**

Executive summary level: One-screen view showing business health, key metrics, and critical alerts. Designed for daily consumption in under two minutes.

Detailed metrics level: Expandable sections showing comprehensive metric breakdowns. Designed for weekly review and drill-down exploration.

Analytical views level: Full analytical interfaces with filtering, segmentation, and comparison capabilities. Designed for deep analysis when investigating specific questions.

**Dashboard Elements**

Health indicators: Visual summary of business health using traffic light colors with clear labels. Not just red/green/yellow but "On track," "Needs attention," "Requires action."

Metric cards: Individual metrics displayed prominently with current value, trend indicator, and target comparison. Designed for quick scanning.

Charts and graphs: Visualizations appropriate to data type—line charts for trends, bar charts for comparisons, pie charts for composition. Include clear labels and annotations.

Alert panels: Prioritized alerts with clear impact statement and recommended action. Designed for quick identification of what needs attention.

**Progressive Disclosure**

Simple view by default: The initial dashboard view shows only essential information.  
Advanced options available: Clicking any section reveals deeper detail.  
Complexity on demand: Users can access full analytical capabilities when needed without being overwhelmed initially.

### 6.3 Notification Patterns

Notifications keep business owners informed without creating alert fatigue.

**Notification Types**

Critical alerts: Require immediate attention—stockouts, significant performance drops, large orders requiring approval. Delivered via all available channels with persistent follow-up until acknowledged.

Important alerts: Require attention within 24 hours—opportunities to optimize, competitive actions, scheduled tasks. Delivered via primary channel with summary digest for lower-priority items.

Informational updates: Worth knowing but not urgent—weekly report delivery, milestone achievements, completed tasks. Delivered via digest or on-demand.

**Notification Channels**

In-app notifications: Always available within the platform interface.  
Email notifications: Preferred for reports and lower-priority alerts.  
SMS notifications: Reserved for critical alerts requiring immediate attention.  
Push notifications: For mobile users requiring real-time awareness.

**Notification Customization**

Business owners can customize notification preferences: "Only notify me via SMS for stockout risks" or "Send me a daily summary email every morning" or "Don't send notifications for routine performance updates—I check the dashboard instead."

The system learns from behavior over time, reducing notifications for patterns the business owner consistently ignores while escalating patterns that consistently require attention.

### 6.4 Feedback and Correction Mechanisms

Business owners can correct system outputs and provide feedback that improves future interactions.

**Correction Mechanisms**

Data corrections: Business owners can correct data errors: "That order was canceled—remove it from my totals" or "The product is actually out of stock, not available." Corrections are applied immediately and used to improve data quality.

Analysis corrections: Business owners can challenge analytical conclusions: "That competitor analysis is wrong—they're not our competitor" or "The ROAS calculation should include revenue from email." Corrections are noted and used to improve future analysis.

Recommendation feedback: Business owners can provide feedback on recommendations: "This recommendation doesn't make sense for my business" or "This worked great—use similar recommendations in the future." Feedback is used to personalize future recommendations.

**Feedback Submission**

Feedback is submitted through natural language: "That recommendation doesn't apply to my situation because we're a B2B business, not B2C" or "The ad copy you generated doesn't match our brand voice—here's what we use instead." The system acknowledges feedback and confirms how it will be used.

## 7. Exit/Scale Flow

The exit/scale flow defines how business owners expand their use of the platform, add team members, and integrate new data sources. This flow recognizes that successful users naturally want to do more with the platform while ensuring they can also export data and transition gracefully if needed.

### 7.1 Platform Expansion

Business owners expand their platform usage as they recognize value and have new needs.

**Feature Discovery**

The system proactively suggests expanded capabilities: "Now that you've seen how I help with campaign management, I can also help with: 1) Inventory optimization—predicting stock needs and preventing stockouts. 2) Customer segmentation—identifying high-value customer groups for targeted marketing. 3) Competitive monitoring—alerting you to competitor pricing and promotions. Which would be most valuable?"

Feature discovery occurs at contextually appropriate moments—when discussing inventory issues, reviewing customer data, or monitoring competitive activity—rather than as a generic upsell.

**Usage Growth**

As business owners use more features, the system tracks adoption and suggests next steps: "You've been using campaign management for 6 weeks now. Based on your progress, you're ready to: 1) Set up automated optimization rules for your ads. 2) Expand to additional advertising platforms. 3) Add predictive forecasting for your campaigns. Would you like to explore any of these?"

### 7.2 Team Scaling

Business owners add team members as their organization grows.

**Team Onboarding**

Team members are invited through simple email invitations: "Invite your team member" followed by email address and role selection. Role options include:

Viewer: Can view dashboards, reports, and insights but cannot make changes.  
Analyst: Can access all data and create reports but cannot modify campaigns or spend budget.  
Manager: Can manage campaigns, approve content, and modify budgets within limits.  
Admin: Full access including team management and settings.

**Team Permissions**

Permissions are managed through natural language: "Let Sarah manage campaigns but require my approval for any spend over $500" or "Allow Mike to view reports but not see financial data." The system translates these requests into appropriate permission settings.

**Team Activity Visibility**

Business owners can see team activity: "Your marketing manager created 3 new campaigns this week. Your operations lead reviewed inventory alerts and reordered 2 products. Sarah approved 15 pieces of content for publication."

### 7.3 Data Source Integration

Business owners connect additional data sources as their operations expand.

**Integration Recommendations**

The system recommends additional integrations based on usage patterns: "I notice you're running campaigns across Facebook, Google, and TikTok, but you're only tracking Facebook and Google in your dashboard. Connecting TikTok would give you complete performance visibility. Want me to help set that up?"

**Integration Process**

New integrations follow the same frictionless pattern as initial setup: "Connect your TikTok Ads" leads to OAuth flow, data loading, and immediate value demonstration with unified dashboard updates.

**Multi-Store Management**

For business owners with multiple stores or brands, the system supports centralized management: "Add your second Shopify store to manage both brands from one dashboard" or "Consolidate your Amazon and Shopify data into a unified view."

### 7.4 Data Export and Portability

Business owners can export their data at any time.

**Export Options**

Data exports are available through simple requests: "Export all my sales data" or "Download my campaign performance report." Export formats include CSV, Excel, and PDF for reports.

**Full Account Export**

Business owners can request a complete account export: "I want to download all my data" receives a comprehensive export including all historical data, configuration, and insights.

**Integration API**

For advanced users, the system provides API access: "Connect your data warehouse to OpenCode via API for real-time data synchronization." API documentation is available for technical team members.

### 7.5 Graceful Exit

Should business owners choose to stop using the platform, the exit process is straightforward.

**Data Preservation**

Before deactivation, business owners receive clear options: "Before you go, here's what you need to know: 1) Your data will be available for 90 days after deactivation. 2) You can download a complete export now. 3) Your team members will lose access when your account is deactivated."

**Feedback Collection**

The system requests feedback on departure: "We're sorry to see you go. Could you share why you're leaving? Your feedback will help us improve." Options include: found a better solution, too expensive, missing key features, no longer need, or other.

**Easy Return**

Return is made easy: "Your account data is preserved for 90 days. When you're ready to return, just log in and pick up where you left off. No re-setup required."

## 8. Flow Integration and Experience Continuity

The seven flows described above must integrate seamlessly to create a cohesive user experience. This section defines how flows connect and how user context persists across interactions.

### 8.1 Flow Connections

Onboarding connects to daily operations: After onboarding, the business owner immediately sees value through the daily dashboard experience. The goals set during onboarding inform the metrics tracked and insights delivered.

Daily operations connects to campaigns: Insights from daily monitoring naturally lead to campaign requests. The system suggests campaigns based on identified opportunities: "Your ROAS has been declining this week—would you like me to create a campaign to test new creative?"

Campaigns connect to decision-making: Campaign results inform strategic decisions. The system connects campaign learnings to broader business decisions: "Your recent campaign tested that sustainability messaging works—want to apply this to your product positioning strategy?"

Decision-making connects to reporting: Decisions made with system support are logged and included in reports. The system tracks decision outcomes and reports progress: "The price increase decision from last month is performing as expected—3% above predicted margin improvement."

Reporting connects to team collaboration: Reports are shared with team members who can contribute insights. Team feedback on reports informs future analysis.

Team collaboration connects to platform scaling: Team needs drive platform expansion—additional features, integrations, and permissions as the team grows.

### 8.2 Context Persistence

User context persists across sessions and flows:

Goal context: Business goals set during onboarding remain active, with continuous tracking and progress reporting. Goals can be updated through natural language: "We exceeded our Q4 revenue goal—let's set a more aggressive target for Q1."

Brand context: Brand preferences, voice, and style guidelines persist across all content generation. Updates to brand context apply immediately to new content: "Update our brand voice to be more playful and casual."

Historical context: Past campaigns, decisions, and outcomes remain accessible for reference and learning. The system references past experiences: "Similar to your spring collection campaign, which achieved 3.2x ROAS..."

Relationship context: The system remembers business owner preferences and patterns: "You usually review campaigns on Monday mornings" or "You prefer detailed analysis before campaign approval."

### 8.3 Experience Consistency

All flows maintain consistent experience characteristics:

Language: Business-friendly terminology throughout—no technical jargon, no translation required.  
Tone: Professional but approachable, like a knowledgeable business advisor rather than a technical tool.  
Pace: Respectful of business owner time—quick answers by default, depth available on request.  
Confidence: Honest about uncertainty, clear about what the system knows versus assumes.  
Action-orientation: Every interaction ends with clear next steps or confirmed confidence in direction.
